When Your System Fails in Ann Arbor

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer prompt, flexible scheduling for most situations. When you call, you'll speak with a real person who will get a technician dispatched to your property without delay. Arrival in the area is typically within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is exactly the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that lights and then shuts down. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip every cycle, plugged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls get fixed on the first trip, at a price you approve before we start.

A few minutes of caution while you wait for us can prevent a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us immediately.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Ann Arbor

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
